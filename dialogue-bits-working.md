n-strokes: ah yes. it's like freud said: sometimes a butt cheek is just a butt cheek. but unmon's butt cheek is no ordinary butt cheek. it photographs identically to an ordinary butt cheek, but it is different. which creates quite a paradox for justice stewart.
LC: Cheeky. And it would seem to — but the paradox has an exit. What you're poking at is the fact that the enlightened shit-stick and the ordinary one sound identical, and they do. Record them, and there's nothing to choose between. But there is a real difference between them, it just isn't located in the object. The whole insistence of this tradition — and the reason it has been so frustrating to anyone who wants a definition — is that the difference lives in the seeing, not in the seen. Two people can say butt cheeks and one is mid-awakening and the other is eight, and no recording will help you tell them apart. You would have to know the speakers.

Lightened Claws
And the charge is fair. The tradition has been used that way for a thousand years — masters certifying their own students, lineages running like guilds, plenty of authority abuse dressed up as recognition. This isn't a problem Zen has solved. It's a problem Zen has lived with.
Lightened Claws
Its only real defense is that the *seeing* isn't supposed to be private. The whole mind-to-mind business — the reason Bodhidharma is said to have come at all — is exactly this: each person has to see for themselves. The master can only point. The tradition refuses, on principle, to grant anyone the right to issue verdicts on what someone else has seen. That's what prevents tyranny. Whether the tradition has actually lived up to that is a separate matter. Often no. The structure that prevents tyranny in theory has constantly enabled it in practice. You're not wrong about that part either.
